WebGL canvas compositing diagnostic — LOCH box investigation

Two identical WebGL canvases draw the same stylized LOCH word with a stylized reveal (L first, then O·C·H, then hold). They differ in exactly one context attribute. The checkerboard is the page behind them: any opaque rectangular region will cover it. This tests what the compositor shows, not framebuffer values.

What to look for on the iPhone
  1. During each cycle, watch the moment the L appears (~the first second). In production, the report is that a rectangular box already surrounds the L at that moment.
  2. Switch to Freeze: L only. Compare A and B: behind the L, do gaps/space stay checkerboard in BOTH canvases, or does one canvas show a dark slab covering the checker inside its dashed outline?
  3. Switch to Freeze: full word. Same check at settle: inside the O counter, in the C mouth, between letters. A black/bright rectangular slab covering the checker within the dashed outline = that context is being composited opaque.
  4. Read the granted attribute line under each canvas: if Safari reports a granted premultipliedAlpha different from what was requested, record it.

Outcomes: A shows the slab, B does not → consistent with the leading hypothesis (premultipliedAlpha:false composited opaquely on this device). Both identical on checker → the isolated compositor behaviour is correct on this device and the production box must have another source. Do not treat this page as the production renderer: confirmation of the production bug additionally requires the real site on the same device.
